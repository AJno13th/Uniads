# Student stories — UGC and testimonial capture

UNIADS' biggest competitive gap is social proof. A competitor publishes **95
Google reviews at 4.9/5**, independently verified, and it is comfortably their
strongest asset. UNIADS currently publishes none.

This is the one thing that cannot be self-produced or written. It needs real
students, on the record, with documented consent.

**Until a student has been captured under the process below, no testimonial
content ships.** No stock footage of graduates implied to be UNIADS students, no
composite students, no "based on a real student" scripts, no AI voices. See
[../guardrails.md](../guardrails.md) section 2.9.

---

## The compliance floor

Four CAP Code rules govern this. They are not optional and they are the reason the
consent form exists.

| Rule | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **CAP 3.47** | Hold documentary evidence the testimonial is genuine, and contact details for the person giving it |
| **CAP 3.50** | No testimonial without permission |
| **CAP 3.49** | Factual claims inside a testimonial must not mislead — a student saying something inaccurate does not make it publishable |
| **CAP 3.45** | Disclose where a review has been incentivised |

That last one matters: **if you give a student anything** — a voucher, a gift, a
discount, a prize entry — the post must say so. The cleanest route is to give
nothing and ask properly.

Note also that a student saying "UNIADS got me £16,000" is *their* mistake and
*your* breach. Factual errors in testimonials must be cut, not published with a
disclaimer.

## Who to ask

In priority order:

1. A student who enrolled with no formal qualifications at 21+ — proves the core claim
2. A student parent using the Childcare Grant — the least-served, highest-empathy story
3. A career changer in their 30s or 40s
4. Someone who was refused or ineligible and was told so honestly — the most credible story you will ever publish, and no competitor will ever run it

Ask people who have already said something kind unprompted. Never ask someone
mid-application — they may feel their outcome depends on saying yes.

## How to ask

Send this, unedited:

> Hi [Name] — would you be up for a short video about your experience with us?
> Two or three minutes on your phone, filmed wherever you like. No script, and you
> can say whatever you actually think, including the parts that were hard.
>
> It would go on our TikTok, Instagram and Facebook. You can see it before it goes
> anywhere, and you can pull it at any point afterwards, no reason needed.
>
> There's no payment and nothing changes for you either way — if it's a no, that's
> genuinely fine and it won't affect anything.

Do not offer a reward. Do not chase more than once.

## Filming brief for the student

Send this with the ask. It is written to be forwarded as-is.

> **Filming your bit — takes about 5 minutes**
>
> - Film on your phone, held **upright**. Prop it against something rather than holding it.
> - Somewhere quiet with a window in front of you, not behind you.
> - Sit close enough that your head and shoulders fill the screen.
> - Don't worry about looking polished. Honestly, rough looks more real and does better.
> - Answer the questions below out loud, one at a time. Leave a two-second gap between each so we can cut them apart.
> - If you fumble, just start that answer again. We'll use the better one.
> - Please don't mention specific amounts of money — we have to be careful about quoting figures, so we'll add any numbers ourselves.
> - Send it over WhatsApp or however's easiest.

## The questions

Six questions. The answers become the posts.

1. **"Where were you before this? What were you doing?"** — establishes the starting point
2. **"What was the thing that stopped you going to university earlier?"** — the objection, in their own words. This is the most valuable answer you'll get.
3. **"What did you assume you needed that it turned out you didn't?"** — hits the Tier 1 hook directly
4. **"What was the bit you were most worried about, and what actually happened?"** — the anxiety beat from SB-10
5. **"What are you studying now, and what's the plan?"** — forward motion
6. **"If you could say one thing to someone sitting where you were a year ago, what is it?"** — almost always the best clip

Ask nothing else. Do not ask "how did UNIADS help you?" — it produces an advert,
and adverts don't get shared.

## What you must not do

- Do not write them a script
- Do not send them lines to hit
- Do not ask for a retake "with more energy about the service"
- Do not stitch two students into one story
- Do not use footage from someone who asked to be removed, including posts already live
- Do not show a student's documents, address or student finance correspondence

## Consent form

Get this in writing — a WhatsApp message with the wording below is sufficient
evidence for CAP 3.47, and screenshot it into the student's CRM record.

> **Video consent — UNIADS**
>
> I, **[full name]**, agree that UNIADS Educational Consulting may use the video I
> recorded on **[date]** on its website and social media accounts, including TikTok,
> Instagram and Facebook.
>
> - I recorded this voluntarily. I was not paid or given anything in return.
> - What I said is true and my own opinion.
> - I understand it may be edited for length, and that subtitles may be added.
> - I can ask for it to be taken down at any time, by messaging [contact], and UNIADS will remove it from its own channels within 5 working days.
> - I am 18 or over.
>
> Name: ______   Date: ______   Contact: ______   Course / institution: ______

Record alongside it, in the CRM:

- Date received and the channel it came through
- Confirmation nothing was given in exchange
- Which posts used the footage, with links
- Any takedown request and the date actioned

**Retention:** keep the consent record for as long as the content is live, plus
two years.

## Editing rules

- Cut for length and clarity only. Never reorder words to change meaning.
- No added claim in the caption that the student did not make on camera
- Name and course on screen (first name is fine): `Aisha, BA Business Management with Foundation, Birmingham`. Vague attribution is what makes a competitor's testimonials read as invented.
- Subtitles burned in, checked by hand
- If they mention a money figure, cut it. Add the correct figure yourself from [../brand-facts.md](../brand-facts.md) as an on-screen note if it's needed.
- If they say something factually wrong — "it's free", "they guarantee your place" — cut it. Do not caption around it.

## Turning one student into several posts

Each student should yield at least three posts:

| Post | Source |
| --- | --- |
| The objection | Q2 + Q3, 15s, TikTok and Reels |
| The worry | Q4, 15s, softer, built to be sent |
| The advice | Q6, 12s, works as a carousel quote card too |

## If you have no students yet

Then ship no testimonial content, and fill the trust slot with things that are
verifiable today:

- SB-09, the named counsellor with the public verification link
- SB-05, the accuracy angle — being the one who quotes £14,135 rather than £16,000 is itself a trust signal
- Screen recordings of GOV.UK as proof of the figures

And start asking for Google reviews now, separately from video. A competitor's 95
reviews took time to accumulate; the sooner that starts, the sooner it stops being
a gap.
