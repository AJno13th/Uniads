## Facebook

**Post at 19:30.** Facebook skews older here, which is the highest-intent segment
— the 30–50s deciding whether university is still realistic. Longer copy works;
it is the one platform where explanation outperforms the hook.

**Format:** text post with the GOV.UK link. No image needed — a link preview from
gov.uk carries more credibility than a graphic.

**Copy**

> **If you're thinking about a January start, read this before you apply anywhere.**
>
> Student finance in England has changed for courses beginning on or after 1 January 2027. The new system is called the Lifelong Learning Entitlement, and applications for it opened this month.
>
> Three things worth knowing:
>
> **1. There's now a lifetime cap on tuition fee loans.** £39,160 in total, which is roughly four years of full-time study at the current maximum fee. That's a lifetime total, not a per-course amount.
>
> **2. If you've studied before, it comes off that total.** The government's own example: someone who completed a three-year degree has £9,790 of entitlement left. That's one year of funding, not four.
>
> **3. Maintenance loans are being extended** to all designated in-person courses from January 2027. You'd normally need to be studying at least 120 credits a year to get the full amount, and the amount still depends on your household income.
>
> Why we're posting this: a lot of what's being advertised right now for January intakes was written for the old system. If you've had student finance before, the honest answer is that it depends on how much of your entitlement is left — and that needs checking properly, not guessing.
>
> We'll check yours with you, free, before you apply anywhere. Message us or call 07368 218457.
>
> Details: https://www.gov.uk/student-finance-on-or-after-1-january-2027

**CTA:** WhatsApp 07368 218457 / Message the page

**No hashtags.** They don't aid reach on Facebook and they make the post look
like a repurposed Instagram caption.
