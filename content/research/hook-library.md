# Hook library

Hook patterns ranked by how much real evidence sits behind them, drawn from
[competitor-teardown.md](competitor-teardown.md). This is the menu the daily
content generator picks from. It is not a list of things that sound good — the
tiers below are strictly about evidence.

**How to read the tiers**

- **Tier 1 — Validated by sustained ad spend.** A competitor has kept this live
  for months across multiple creatives. Advertisers kill losers in days, so
  months of continuous spend is the strongest signal available to us.
- **Tier 2 — Validated by measured organic reach.** Supported by view/share
  numbers we pulled directly.
- **Tier 3 — Validated by demand signals.** Backed by competitor SEO targeting or
  by recurring themes in their verified customer reviews. Real audience intent,
  but not yet proven as a video hook.
- **Tier 4 — Untested.** Reasoned from the offer. Use sparingly and watch results.

Every hook must be paired with a fact from [brand-facts.md](../brand-facts.md)
and pass [guardrails.md](../guardrails.md) before it ships.

## Tier 1 — Validated by sustained ad spend

### 1.1 The qualification objection
Seven months continuously live for UNISEF (from 9 Feb 2026, 8 creatives, re-run
14 Feb – 17 Jun 2026 with 4 more), and present as far back as Oct 2025. The most
validated hook in this market by a wide margin.

- "🎓 No A Levels? No Problem!" — their wording
- UNIADS' on-brand version, already live on site: **"No GCSEs? No A-Levels? No problem."**

Variations to rotate so it never repeats verbatim:

- "You don't need A-Levels. You need to be 21."
- "I left school at 16. I start my degree in January."
- "The qualification you think you need? You don't."
- "Everyone thinks you need A-Levels for uni. Here's what you actually need."

Why it works: it names the exact belief blocking the viewer, and it is the
objection that stops people before they even search.

### 1.2 Time compression
UNISEF runs three separate time-compression angles concurrently.

- "🎓 Uni Qualification in just one year?" (CertHE, live from 16 May 2026, 8 creatives)
- "Wait — why are thousands of working adults suddenly doing this one-year uni course?" (live from 9 Feb 2026, 8 creatives)
- "Did you know you could get a university degree in just two years?"

For UNIADS this maps onto genuine partner provision: CertHE (1 year), HND
(2 years), top-up routes, and the foundation-year route where you are in higher
education from day one.

### 1.3 Keep your job
Runs through almost every UNISEF ad as the reassurance beat.

- "You don't need to quit your job to get a university degree."
- "You don't have to pause your life to change your future."

UNIADS fact behind it: 2 days a week, with morning, evening and weekend options.

### 1.4 Career outcome with a number
"Careers in Psychology can pay up to £60,000 a year (or more!)" — live from
16 May 2026 across 4 creatives.

UNIADS can only use this with a cited, dated salary source (ONS, NHS pay bands,
National Careers Service). No invented figures. See guardrails.

### 1.5 The psychology / counselling vertical
Not a hook so much as a proven *subject*. UNISEF launched four separate psychology
ads between Mar and May 2026 — that is what finding a winner looks like. The
framing is consistently: help people, keep working, real career ceiling,
experience counts as entry evidence.

UNIADS has psychology-with-counselling routes among its partners, so this vertical
is pre-validated and currently uncontested by Just Educated.

### 1.6 The rule change nobody has covered
Not validated by competitor spend — validated by its **absence** from it. The
student finance system changes for courses starting on or after 1 January 2027
(the Lifelong Learning Entitlement), and applications opened in September 2026.

None of the 19 competitor ads captured mentions it. Neither competitor site
mentions it. Both are advertising January intakes using old-system framing.

This is the strongest single opportunity in the research: a genuine, sourceable,
timely change that directly affects the exact intake being sold, where the whole
category is currently wrong.

- "Student finance changed in January. Nobody told you."
- "If your course starts in January 2027, the rules are different. Here's how."
- "There's now a lifetime cap on your tuition loan. £39,160."
- "Studied before? That comes off your entitlement now."

Handle with care — see [brand-facts.md](../brand-facts.md) section 6b. One fact
per post, source on screen, and never apply it to an individual's circumstances.
The prior-study rule in particular must route to a human, because UNIADS' own
4-year foundation-year offer costs exactly the full entitlement.

## Tier 2 — Validated by measured organic reach

### 2.1 Short, single-idea, built to be sent
The one video in our sample that reached six figures was **12 seconds** long with
one idea and one CTA (985,900 plays). The two that died were 22s and 50s with
feature-list captions (832 and 252 plays).

Design rule, not a hook: **12–20 seconds, one idea, one CTA.**

### 2.2 Build for shares, not comments
The 985k video earned 614 shares against 32 comments — roughly 19 shares per
comment. Distribution in this niche comes from one person sending a video to
someone who "should see this": a partner, a sibling, a friend stuck in a job.

So write to the sender, not just the viewer:

- "Send this to someone who thinks they left it too late."
- "Tag the friend who keeps saying they wish they'd gone to uni."
- "Someone in your family needs to see this."

### 2.3 Myth-correction framing
"The biggest lie about university? That it takes over your whole life."
(`@ukplatinumservices`) — the account is a weak performer overall, but the
pattern-interrupt structure is sound and matches the Tier 1 objection hooks.

## Tier 3 — Validated by demand signals

### 3.1 Age-anchored
Straight from Just Educated's SEO targeting, where real search volume is:
`study-in-the-uk-at-21`, `how-to-apply-university-at-30-uk`,
`uk-business-degree-for-adult-learners`,
`best-university-courses-for-mature-students-uk`.

- "Starting a degree at 30 isn't late. Here's the maths."
- "You're 35. You'll graduate at 39. Then what — 25 more working years?"
- "Am I too old for university?" (answer it in 12 seconds)

The word "mature student" is the industry's word, not the audience's. People
search their own age. Use the number.

### 3.2 The funding-eligibility question
The genuinely under-served question in this market. Nobody is answering it
properly, and UNIADS already collects exactly these signals in its qualifier:
settlement status, length of UK residency, prior study, age band.

- "Settled status but never studied here — do you get student finance?"
- "Three years in the UK. That's the number that decides your maintenance loan."
- "You've had student finance before. Here's what that actually blocks."

### 3.3 The fear-and-anxiety beat
Direct from Just Educated's 95 verified Google reviews, where the recurring
emotional theme is anxiety removal — "As someone who tends to overthink decisions
like this, let me just say, GO FOR IT."

- "You're not confused. Nobody explains this properly."
- "The bit people are actually scared of isn't the studying."
- "You've had the tab open for two years, haven't you."

### 3.4 One named human
Their reviews name "Sameul" repeatedly and credit him personally. People buy an
advisor, not an agency. UNIADS has a stronger version of this available: a named
counsellor with a verifiable British Council credential and a public verification
URL. No competitor publishes a named, credentialled, accountable person.

### 3.5 Student-parent angle
Childcare Grant plus Parents' Learning Allowance is a real, specific,
non-repayable entitlement that barely features in competitor content. Just
Educated mentions it once in an FAQ; UNISEF not at all. UNIADS has a whole service
page for it.

- "You have a two-year-old. That's not the reason you can't go — it's £199.62 a week towards childcare."

## Tier 4 — Untested

Reasoned from the offer, no external validation yet. Cap at roughly one in five
posts and judge on results.

- The accuracy wedge: "Someone told you £16,000 a year. The actual maximum is £14,135. Here's why that matters." — strong differentiator, but it is competitor-adjacent, so keep it about the number and never name a competitor.
- No-UCAS relief: "You don't have to touch UCAS."
- Geography: naming a specific campus city to a local audience.
- Day-in-the-life of a 2-day-a-week timetable.

## Anti-patterns — measured failures

Do not do these. Each is a format we watched underperform.

| Anti-pattern | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Caption as feature list with emoji bullets, several phone numbers and 20+ hashtags | Both sub-1k-view videos in our sample |
| Videos over ~30 seconds for cold audiences | 50s video → 252 plays |
| Posting volume as a strategy | 438 videos → 832 plays on a recent post |
| Hashtag spam contradicting the offer | Just Educated's Meta ads used `#InternationalStudents`, `#StudyAbroad`, `#UKStudentVisa` on a home-fee Student-Finance offer |
| Generic aspiration with no specific claim | "Join university🎓 To Unlock Your Potential – Apply Now!" ran 7 days and was never revived |
| Round-number funding claims | "up to £16,000" exceeds the actual £14,135 maximum — an ASA exposure, not a benefit |

## Rotation rule

The daily generator must not reuse a hook family on consecutive days, and must
not reuse a specific hook within 30 days. Tier mix per week, so that most output
sits on proven ground while still testing:

- Tier 1: 3 of 7 days
- Tier 2 framing: applies to every video as a craft rule, not a slot
- Tier 3: 3 of 7 days
- Tier 4: 1 of 7 days

`../posted-log.md` is the record of what has already run.
