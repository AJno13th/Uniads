# Guardrails — what must never appear in a UNIADS post

Two jobs. First, stop the daily generator inventing facts (the reason this system
produces one day at a time against a locked fact sheet instead of 90 posts in one
go). Second, keep UNIADS on the right side of the UK advertising rules in a
category where competitors are visibly not.

Read alongside [brand-facts.md](brand-facts.md). A post that passes the checklist
at the bottom can ship; anything else gets rewritten.

---

## 1. The hard rule

> **If a claim is not in `brand-facts.md`, or on a GOV.UK page cited in it, it
> does not go in a post.**

No exceptions for "obviously true", "roughly right", or "the competitor says it so
it must be fine". A competitor publishing a number is not substantiation — see
section 4, where one of them publishes a figure above the legal maximum.

## 2. Banned claims

### 2.1 Any statistic about UNIADS
UNIADS publishes no statistics. Therefore no post may state or imply:

- a number of students helped, enrolled or placed
- a student finance approval rate ("98% approval")
- an admissions or pass rate ("94% pass rate")
- a satisfaction score, star rating or review count
- years in business
- a number of campuses

Under CAP 3.7 a marketer must **hold documentary evidence before publication** for
any claim consumers would treat as objective. UNIADS holds none of these, so they
cannot be published — regardless of whether they are true.

### 2.2 Guarantees and speed promises
Never: "guaranteed place", "guaranteed funding", "you will be approved", "offer in
48 hours", "we'll get you in".

UNIADS does not control admissions decisions or Student Finance England
assessments. Promising either is an exaggeration of capability under CAP 3.11.

Write "we check your eligibility properly before you apply", not "you qualify".

### 2.3 "Free university" / "100% free education"
This is the single biggest mis-selling risk in the category, and competitors walk
straight into it.

- **Free:** UNIADS' counselling, application support and student finance guidance.
- **Not free:** tuition. It is covered by a *Tuition Fee Loan* which the student
  repays.

Banned: "free degree", "100% free education", "university for free", "fully funded"
used bare, "no cost to you" about tuition.

Approved: "Our support is free." / "Tuition is covered by a tuition fee loan, which
you repay once you're earning above the threshold."

Every post that mentions tuition being covered must say, in the post itself, that
it is a loan. Under CAP 3.9 a qualification cannot rescue a claim it contradicts,
so "free" plus small print does not work — don't make the claim.

### 2.4 Funding figures above the published maximum
Never state a maintenance loan figure above **£14,135** (2026/27, living away from
home in London). A competitor advertises "up to £16,000 per year". Copying that
would breach CAP 3.11 and, since it would affect a decision to commit to a course,
CAP 20.9.

Any maintenance loan figure must carry both:
1. "up to", and
2. a household-income qualifier — most students receive less than the maximum.

### 2.5 Student finance as a UNIADS benefit
Student finance is a statutory entitlement from the UK government, not something
UNIADS provides.

> **CAP 3.12** — "Marketing communications must not present rights given to
> consumers in law as a distinctive feature of the marketer's offer."

Banned: "we get you £14,135", "we fund your degree", "we give you a maintenance
loan", "our funding".

Approved: "You may be eligible for a maintenance loan of up to £X. We help you
apply for it."

### 2.6 Misrepresenting the credential
The badge is **training held by a named individual**: "UK Knowledge Trained
Counsellor", British Council Agent and Counsellor Training Hub, held by Anthony
Joshua, valid to 25 June 2027.

Banned: "British Council accredited", "British Council approved", "British Council
partner", "endorsed by the British Council", or any wording implying the British
Council backs UNIADS as a company.

> **CAP 3.52** — communications must not display a trust or quality mark without
> authorisation, or claim approval or endorsement by any body that has not given
> it. The ASA upheld exactly this against A.R Recruitment Consultancy
> (t/a Soft Skills Courses) alongside a misleading professional-status claim.

Approved: "Our counsellor is British Council trained — here's the badge, verify it
yourself" plus the verification URL.

### 2.7 Misrepresenting what the qualification is
Name the actual award. A CertHE is a Certificate of Higher Education, not "a
degree". An HND is a Higher National Diploma — equivalent to the first two years
of a degree, requiring a top-up year to become a full bachelor's.

> The ASA ruled against London School of Planning and Management for advertising
> an "MBA" that was in fact a Level 7 Diploma requiring a separate paid top-up
> elsewhere — breaching CAP 3.1, 3.3 and 3.9.

Banned: calling a foundation year "a degree", calling an HND "a degree", implying a
top-up is automatic or included when it is a separate application.

Approved: "A CertHE is a full year of university study you can build into a degree,
or stop after and still hold the qualification."

### 2.8 Overclaiming employment outcomes

> **CAP 20.9** — ads for vocational training or instruction courses "must not give
> a misleading impression about the potential for employment that might follow",
> and must make clear significant conditions for acceptance such as level of
> attainment, and conditions affecting the decision such as cost or duration.

Salary figures are allowed only with a named, dated, authoritative source — ONS,
NHS pay bands, or the National Careers Service — cited on screen. A competitor's
"Careers in Psychology can pay up to £60,000 a year" is not a source.

Banned: "this degree gets you a job", "£60k after graduating", any implied job
guarantee.

Approved: "Health and social care roles are advertised at £X–£Y on the NHS pay
bands (Band 5, 2026/27)" — with the band visible on screen.

Posts about a specific course should also state its duration, and that entry
depends on the partner's requirements.

### 2.9 Fake or unsourced social proof

- **CAP 3.47** — hold documentary evidence a testimonial is genuine, and contact details for the person giving it.
- **CAP 3.50** — no testimonial without permission.
- **CAP 3.49** — factual claims inside a testimonial must not mislead.
- **CAP 3.45** — disclose where reviews have been incentivised.

Banned: invented students, stock-photo "graduates" presented as UNIADS students,
AI-generated testimonials, paraphrasing a real student into a stronger claim than
they made, reusing a competitor's review.

A competitor's site carries testimonials attributed to "James Anderson, Student"
and "Emma Thompson, Student" with no course, university or photo. Do not imitate
that. Use the consent process in [filming/ugc-brief.md](filming/ugc-brief.md).

### 2.10 Eligibility promises to individuals
Never tell a commenter or viewer they qualify. Eligibility depends on settlement
status, three years' continuous UK residency, the provider, the course, previous
study and age.

Approved reply pattern: "It depends on your status and how long you've been in the
UK — send us a message and we'll check it properly before you apply."

### 2.11 Wrong-audience signals
Do not use `#StudyAbroad`, `#InternationalStudents`, `#UKStudentVisa`,
`#StudyInUK` or similar. UNIADS' offer is home-fee, Student-Finance-funded study
for people already settled in the UK. A competitor's Meta ads used all four on a
home-fee offer — it misleads applicants who cannot access the funding, and wastes
reach.

### 2.12 Competitor naming
Never name, tag, screenshot or subtweet Just Educated, UNISEF or any other
consultancy. The accuracy wedge is fought on the number, not the rival: "You might
have seen £16,000 quoted. The published maximum is £14,135."

## 3. Tone rules

- No fake urgency. "Applications close tonight" is only usable if a real deadline exists that day.
- No shaming. The audience is adults who left school early, often with children and jobs. Never imply they wasted time or failed.
- Say "you", not "mature students". "Mature student" is the sector's word; people search their own age.
- Plain English. No "pathway provision", "articulation agreement", "HE provider".
- One idea per post.

## 4. Where competitors get it wrong — do not copy

| Competitor practice | Why it is off-limits |
| --- | --- |
| "maintenance loans up to £16,000 per year" | Above the £14,135 maximum. CAP 3.11 |
| "98% approval rate", "94% admissions pass rate" | No substantiation. CAP 3.7 |
| "600+ successful enrolments", "6+ years in business" | Fine for them if evidenced; UNIADS has no equivalent figure, so cannot mirror it |
| "100% free education" | Conflates free service with repayable tuition loan. CAP 3.1, 3.9 |
| "offer letter within 48 hours" | Outcome the advertiser does not control. CAP 3.11 |
| Testimonials with no course, university or photo | CAP 3.47, 3.50 |
| `#InternationalStudents` on a home-fee offer | CAP 3.1 and self-defeating targeting |
| Contradictory numbers on one page (595+ / 638+ / 1362+ students; 9+ / 14+ campuses) | Destroys trust and is unsubstantiated either way |
| Publishing "51% Satisfaction" as a selling point | Self-inflicted; just don't publish stats you don't have |

## 5. Pre-publish checklist

Every post, every platform. A post failing any line gets rewritten, not softened.

1. Is every factual claim traceable to `brand-facts.md` or a GOV.UK page cited there?
2. Are all numbers exact, with "up to" and a household-income qualifier where relevant?
3. If tuition is mentioned as covered, does the post say it is a **loan**?
4. Does the post avoid guaranteeing a place, funding or a job?
5. Is the credential described as *training held by a named counsellor*, never as British Council endorsement of UNIADS?
6. Is the award named accurately (CertHE / HND / foundation year / degree)?
7. Any salary figure sourced and dated on screen?
8. Any testimonial real, consented and evidenced?
9. Does it avoid promising eligibility to an individual?
10. Are hashtags home-student appropriate — no study-abroad or visa tags?
11. Is any urgency real?
12. Is no competitor named or shown?
13. Is it one idea, and under 20 seconds if video?
14. Has this hook been used in the last 30 days? Check [posted-log.md](posted-log.md).

## 6. Escalate to a human

Stop and flag rather than publish if a post would need to:

- state a student finance repayment threshold (it changes — verify on GOV.UK that day)
- confirm whether a specific immigration status qualifies
- claim a specific partner will accept applicants with no qualifications, where `src/data/universities.ts` says otherwise
- respond to a comment describing personal financial hardship
- make any claim about a named partner institution's accreditation
